Architecture

Why do we use Helper Notebooks?

Centralized Logic Instead of writing custom error tracking and logging in every Silver/Gold notebook, we maintain the logic centrally in the fabric/Monitoring/Audit/ module.
Your Responsibility as a Developer Because the logic is external, you must actively invoke these helpers inside your layer notebook using try/except blocks to ensure your pipeline doesn't become a "silent failure zone".
Step 1

Start the Audit Session

At the very beginning of your notebook, initialize tracking so the platform knows your process has started.

%run nb_insert_audit_table_session
What happens inside the helper? The helper automatically generates a new record in the log.audit_table_session tracking database, capturing the current timestamp, your layer, and the table you are modifying.
Step 2

Logging Invalid Records

During your transformation process, if you encounter malformed data, do not drop it silently.

log_invalid_record(dataframe, ...)
What happens inside the helper? The helper safely formats the bad row and appends it to the centralized log.invalid_record table. This ensures all layers dump their bad data into one easily queryable location for Data Stewards to review.
Step 3

Triggering Error Recovery

If your notebook encounters a fatal exception, catch it and call the update helper before crashing.

%run nb_update_audit_session_and_next_run_mode_dev
What happens inside the helper? 1. It connects to cfg.next_run_mode and sets it to 'RECOVERY' so the orchestrator knows the next run must fix this batch.

2. It updates log.audit_session status to 'FAILED' with the exact error message.

3. It raises the exception to stop the pipeline.
